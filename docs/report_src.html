TerraMind AI

An AI-assisted real estate investment platform that follows the steps a development fund takes to evaluate a project, from zoning capacity to the investment committee and asset management. AI agents research assumptions with sources; a deterministic engine does every calculation.

One sentenceTerraMind turns a land parcel and a few fundamentals into a sourced, auditable underwriting, investment-committee memo and monitoring dashboard in minutes instead of weeks.

1Motivation

Mid-size developers, development funds and family offices decide whether to buy land with spreadsheets, scattered consultant reports and manual research, and the gap between analysis and real conditions costs them time, money and missed projects.

Who has the problem

The user is the investment team of a development fund or a mid-size developer: an acquisitions analyst, an investment director and an investment committee. In Mexico City this is a large, fragmented market. Softec estimates the metro area needs roughly 120,000 homes a year while developers deliver about 40,000 apartments, and land cost and regulation are the main reasons new housing is expensive [1]. Nationally, CONAVI data put the housing backlog at about 8.9 million homes, and about 2.8 million new homes are needed in 2025 alone [2]. Every viable project a team screens faster is supply that can get built.

How they handle it today

  • Zoning: someone checks the SEDUVI map or orders a Certificado Único de Zonificación, then turns codes like H/6/20/Z into buildable area by hand.
  • Market and cost: comparables are copied from listing portals, and cost-per-m² benchmarks come from paid cost guides or a contractor's ballpark.
  • Model: an Excel pro forma (or ARGUS Developer at larger firms) with the debt, sales schedule and waterfall rebuilt for each deal. Assumptions rarely carry a source or date.
  • Committee and monitoring: a memo written in Word, and later a separate tracker comparing actuals to the budget.

What it costs them

Industry estimates put manual deal analysis at 40–80 analyst hours per deal, including memo and deck work [3]. Development deals need even more steps: zoning, design capacity, unit mix and construction schedule. Most screened deals are rejected, so most of this effort is spent on "no". Worse, spreadsheet underwriting fails silently: a hard-coded rate, a missing escalation, interest charged on total cost instead of the actual drawn balance, or an assumption nobody can trace. Errors show up after the land is bought. And sustainability is usually evaluated last, if at all, even though buildings account for roughly a third or more of global energy-related emissions [4].

What TerraMind does differently.

  1. One integrated lifecycle (feasibility → underwriting → financing → sustainability → IC → asset management) on one shared assumption register. Every module reads the same numbers, and changing one input reprices everything.
  2. AI that researches but never calculates. Agents find zoning rules, comparables, cost benchmarks and rates with sources. A tested, deterministic Python engine computes every metric.
  3. Investment-grade governance: every value is tagged user / researched / default / demo, with verification and date. Researched values stay pending until approved. Material changes need a confirmation click, overriding a legal limit flags the scenario as noncompliant, and there is a full audit trail with undo.
  4. Reads your documents: upload a zoning certificate, permit, contract or the developer's Excel model. TerraMind extracts the facts, proposes model updates (checked against the file) and reviews contracts for risks. Projects are private to each account, and guests can use it without signing up.
  5. English or Spanish: one click switches the whole interface (English by default); the agents then answer in Spanish too, and the existing chat conversation is translated.
  6. Mexico-first (CDMX zoning codes, ISAI transfer tax, TIIE, presale payment schedules), with manual fallback anywhere.
  7. Sustainability with the right beneficiary: in build-to-sell, energy savings go to the buyer, so the developer only sees capex and any price premium. In build-to-rent, savings raise NOI and exit value.

Why it matters: faster "no" decisions, fewer silent errors, and a memo whose every number can be traced, which is what investment committees and lenders actually ask for.

4.2 Organization

There are no cycles: specialists have no delegation tool, so delegation depth is 1 by construction. This removes the runaway loops we saw in Homework 5. The confirmation path (dashed) exists only in the UI; the chatbot has no tool for it.

4.3 Models and why

All agents use gpt-6-luna through Portkey, the course model, via the OpenAI Responses API, which this model requires for tool calling. We use PydanticAI with reasoning_effort=low. Specialists use OpenAI's native web search with search_context_size=low. We tested "low" against "medium" on five scripted instructions: both got 5/5 right, and low was 13.5% cheaper ($0.00030 vs $0.00034 per turn) with similar latency, so we kept low (§5.4). The Financial Modeling "agent" is deliberately not an LLM (§4.6).

4.4 A real run, step by step

The following is a live session recorded on 24 Oct 2026 (full transcript and token counts in docs/evidence/chat_tests.md).

  1. Create from a sentence. On the portfolio page the user typed: "I'm evaluating a 2,500 m² residential property in Polanco, Mexico City. The land costs MXN 120 million, and I want to use 60% bank financing." The Investment Manager called create_project, looked up the registry keys, and then update_assumptions. Land area and price were applied. The 60% LTC is a financing commitment, so it was returned pending with a Confirm button. The answer listed the missing inputs instead of inventing them (12.6 s, $0.00045).
  2. Research. The five specialists ran for the Polanco case (9–18 s each). The Regulatory agent refused to invent zoning for a fictional lot: every parameter came back not_found, with the official SEDUVI/SIG portal and the Certificado Único de Zonificación named as the way to verify. On a real address the user entered (Adolfo López Mateos 1964, Los Alpes, Álvaro Obregón) the agent found HM/15/20/Z at corridor level, from an official PAOT resolution for No. 2000 on the same boulevard. It labeled the result preliminary · corridor/colonia, and code derived FAR 12.0 from it. Users can also type the code from their zoning certificate, which TerraMind parses deterministically (Norma 11 density table). The Financing agent found the published TIIE de Fondeo (6.52%) on banxico.org.mx. The Market agent returned listing comparables labeled asking, not transaction, and code computed their median of MXN 101,083/m².
  3. Approve. Researched values appear as AI proposals with source and verification; the user approves or rejects them (Figure 5).
  4. Edit by chat. "Increase construction costs by 10%" → update_assumptions(op=pct): the hard cost per m² goes from MXN 24,000 to 26,400, and the tool returned the engine's before/after metrics (Figure 4).
  5. Ask. "What's the maximum amount we should pay for the land if we want an 18% equity IRR?" → solve_for_target bisects the full model: MXN 318.2M vs the MXN 265M asking price (base case). Nothing is saved.
  6. Decide. The Investment Committee (two challengers in parallel, then the chair) returned "Invest with conditions" for Polanco and "Reject" for the risky Del Valle case, whose base equity IRR (11.5%) is below the 18% hurdle and which breaches the presales covenant.

4.5 The financial engine

A monthly model (end-of-month convention, t = 0 is land closing) covers pre-development, an S-curve construction schedule with monthly cost escalation, presales with deposits, construction-stage installments and closing payments, cancellations, commissions and marketing. For rentals it adds lease-up, NOI, rent growth and a terminal value on forward NOI. The capital stack is equity first, then mezzanine, then senior debt sized on cost including financing (solved iteratively). Interest accrues on the actual monthly balance, which is either capitalized or paid, plus origination and commitment fees. After delivery a sales sweep repays debt, and a balloon or maturity shortfall is flagged as a funding gap. Outputs include TDC, GDV, profit, margins, project and equity IRR, MOIC, NPV, peak funding and equity, LTC, LTV, DSCR, cash-on-cash, covenants and a waterfall (return of capital → compounded pref → promote). Undefined IRRs return undefined, never a number. Beyond scalar inputs, the model accepts time-varying schedules: sales phases (e.g. a launch peak and a delivery peak), price phases, land installments, custom one-off cash flows and the construction spending curve. The chatbot sets them from plain language ("assume a big selling point at the start and end of construction"). The model can be downloaded as a live Excel workbook: every number is a formula linked to one Assumptions tab (development program, construction and sales schedules, cash flow with financing, waterfall, budget, outputs and investor charts). We recalculated it in Microsoft Excel. It matches the engine to within rounding on all demo cases, and an input changed inside Excel (e.g. hard cost +10%) gives exactly the engine's result (docs/evidence/excel_reconciliation.md). Users can also add new variables (e.g. a trust fee as % of sales collections, storage units sold per apartment): each has a defined effect (cost per unit, % of revenue, income per unit, price %…) that the engine applies deterministically, and it appears on the relevant page and in the Assumption Center. Tests check the accounting identities on every demo case: budget lines sum to total cost, unlevered flows sum to revenue minus TDC, equity profit equals development profit, and interest equals balance × rate each month.

4.6 Why the arithmetic is not done by the LLM (measured)

We gave gpt-6-luna the 33 monthly equity cash flows of the Polanco case and asked for the annualized IRR three times. It answered 25.02–25.03%; the engine gives 24.91% on the same flows. That is off by 0.11 points, at about 10 s and $0.0005–0.0006 per calculation, more than an entire chat turn, and not reproducible by construction. The engine is exact and deterministic, runs in about 5 ms and uses zero tokens. Sensitivities, solvers and the unit-mix optimizer (66 full model runs in 0.3 s) would cost dollars per question if an LLM did them. (docs/evidence/llm_math_experiment.json)

5.3 Cost-cutting steps

  • Deterministic engine: metrics, what-ifs, scenarios, solvers and optimizers cost 0 tokens. Asking the LLM to do one IRR costs more than a full chat turn (§4.6).
  • Prompt caching: a long static system prompt and tool schemas come first, with the compact project snapshot in the user message, so 74% of chat input tokens are billed at the cached rate.
  • Compact context: a ~2k-token project snapshot instead of full tables, and only the last 6 chat messages as plain text.
  • Research cache and dedup: identical requests cost $0, and a duplicate request within one turn is blocked.
  • Agents only on demand: one chat agent; specialists run only when asked or when a needed input is empty (≤ 2 per turn). No agent calls another agent in a loop.
  • Hard limits: per-turn caps on specialist calls, tool calls, model requests, tokens and USD. The turn stops and asks permission before spending more (Figure 9).
  • Low reasoning effort and search_context_size=low where quality was unchanged.

7.2 Reach

The first market is Mexico City residential development, which faces a supply gap of ~80,000 homes a year [1]. The same engine covers build-to-rent and acquisitions, and other cities work through researched or manual assumptions. A fund screening 50 sites a year at 40–80 hours each spends 2,000–4,000 analyst hours. Cutting a first screen to a few hours frees most of that for the deals worth pursuing.

7.3 User validation

Status: a structured user test with a practicing real estate investment professional has not been run yet at the time of writing. The figures above come from the system's own measurements and published estimates, not from a user trial. The test protocol is ready: (1) the participant underwrites the Polanco case the way they do today (Excel / usual tools) and times it; (2) they repeat it in TerraMind, editing by chat and form; (3) we record time to a first IC-ready view, the number of assumptions with a traceable source, errors found, and their feedback on what they would and would not trust.
